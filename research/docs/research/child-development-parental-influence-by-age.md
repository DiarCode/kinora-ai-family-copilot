# Parental influence and child "susceptibility" by age (0-18): should a US parenting-skills product stop at 12?

Prepared 2026-11-14. Tags: **[S]** peer-reviewed/primary (abstract or record seen via search; full text not always read), **[SEC]** secondary/commentary, **[M]** from memory, verify before citing. Items marked "(search-confirmed)" were retrieved in this session; others are [M].

## Bottom line

- **0-12 is a defensible core** (attachment, self-regulation, habits, value transmission, and the best-validated behavioral parent training).
- **"Parents matter most before 12" is only partly true.** Evidence supports "parents matter *most intensely and directly*" before about 12. It does not support "parents stop mattering at 13". Adolescence is a second period of high plasticity and risk, and the parent-teen relationship remains a top predictor of teen mental health and substance use.
- **The Heckman-style "earlier is always better" claim is not supported for parenting programs.** The best direct test found no age moderation of parenting-program effects on child conduct problems.
- **Recommendation:** Keep 0-12 as the core, and add 13-18 as a separate later track ("Teens") with a different mechanic (coaching/consultant role, disclosure and relationship, conflict, digital, mental health). If scope is tight, ship 0-12 first and 13-15 second. Do not market the 13+ track as "the same skills, older kid".

## 1. Early childhood: sensitive periods (evidence FOR "early")

## 2. Middle childhood (6-12): values, regulation, habits

- **Value and religion transmission via parents.** Bengtson, *Families and Faith* (Oxford 2013), Longitudinal Study of Generations. About 6 in 10 children kept the parental tradition. Warm relationships (especially with the father) were the strongest predictor of transmission. Authoritarian parenting produced "rebels". Bengtson says parental influence is about the same as in 1970. These are secondary and promotional sources, so check the book. https://www.iclrs.org/blurb/families-and-faith-how-religion-is-passed-down-across-generations ; https://www.christianitytoday.com/2013/09/religion-runs-in-family [SEC]
- **Political orientation.** Jennings, Stoker and Bowers 2009, "Politics Across Generations", *J Politics* 71(3):782-799. Transmission patterns were robust across cohorts. Caveats: later reviews find parent-child similarity weaker than early theory predicted, and some of it is genetic. https://typeset.io/pdf/politics-across-generations-family-transmission-reexamined-1t5la4mjdp.pdf (search-confirmed) [S]/[SEC]
- **Grusec and Goodnow 1994 (value internalization).** Internalization depends on the child accurately perceiving the message and accepting it. *Developmental Psychology* 30(1):4-19. [M] Verify the DOI.
- **Important for the age argument:** the transmission evidence does not show it is *completed* by 12. In Jennings and in Bengtson, the outcome is measured in young adulthood after teen years of relationship. Adolescence is the period in which values are tested.

## 3. Adolescence as a SECOND sensitive period (evidence AGAINST "parents matter most before 12")

| Claim | Evidence | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Adolescence is a window of heightened social/brain plasticity | Dahl, Allen, Wilbrecht and Suleiman 2018, *Nature* 554:441-450, DOI 10.1038/nature25770. Argues for investing in adolescence. Puberty alters learning and plasticity, and early adolescence (10-14) is a pivotal target. https://www.nature.com/articles/nature25770 (search-confirmed) | [S] |
| BUT strong evidence for an adolescent *sensitive period* is lacking | Fuhrmann, Knoll and Blakemore 2015, *Trends Cogn Sci* 19(10):558-566, DOI 10.1016/j.tics.2015.07.008. Concrete evidence is largely lacking. Specific windows within adolescence may be sensitive to specific inputs. | Peers raise risk-taking more in teens than in adults | Gardner and Steinberg 2005, *Dev Psychol* 41(4):625-635, DOI 10.1037/0012-1649.41.4.625. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16060809/ (search-confirmed). The design could not separate peer presence from peer encouragement. | [S] |
| Popular syntheses | Steinberg, *Age of Opportunity* (2014); Blakemore, *Inventing Ourselves* (2018). Books, not searched. | [M] |

Interpretation: neuroscience supports "adolescence is a high-stakes period", not "parents become irrelevant". Fuhrmann 2015 is an honest hedge; use Dahl 2018 for the opportunity framing and cite Fuhrmann for the caveat.

## 4. Do teens still listen to parents?

| Finding | Evidence | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Parent-family connectedness protects against almost every health-risk behavior (emotional distress, suicidality, violence, tobacco, alcohol, marijuana, sexual behaviors), except pregnancy history. Parental expectations for school achievement also protective. N=12,118 in grades 7-12. Cross-sectional, so associational only. | Resnick et al. 1997, *JAMA* 278(10):823-832, DOI 10.1001/jama.1997.03550100049038. https://www.cpc.unc.edu/resources/publications/bib/910/ (search-confirmed) | [S] |

Follow-up: Kerr, Stattin and Burk 2010; a 2009 US sample replication in *J Res Pers* (found via search). | [S] |
| Authoritative parenting predicts better school performance and engagement in adolescents (1992), and better adjustment over a year (1994). Weaker association for some ethnic groups. | Steinberg, Lamborn, Dornbusch and Darling 1992, *Child Dev* 63; Steinberg et al. 1994, *Child Dev* 65:754-770. https://core.ac.uk/download/279072117.pdf (1994, search-confirmed) | [S] |
| Teens still go to parents, but less so as they age. In a 2012 Pew survey on privacy advice, younger teens (12-13) were far more likely than older teens to ask parents (58% vs 33%), and many focus-group teens said they rely on themselves. | Pew 2013 https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2013/08/15/main-findings-4/ (search-confirmed). Dated and topic-specific. | [S]/[SEC] |
| "Teens name parents as the top influence" surveys (e.g., Responsibility.org/Roper on alcohol; Talking Futures UK) | Not verified. No 2023/2024 Pew item found on "who teens turn to for advice". Do not cite a specific number until a primary source is pulled. | [M] |
| Peers dominate short-term/style and risk in the moment; parents dominate long-term values, education, career | Consistent with the sources above (Gardner and Steinberg 2005, Resnick 1997, Jennings 2009), but this exact domain split is a common textbook synthesis (e.g., Harris 1998, Steinberg), not tested here. | [M] |

Counter-view to include honestly: behavior-genetic work and Harris's "group socialization" thesis argue that shared-family-environment effects are smaller than commonly assumed. [M] Verify (Harris 1995, *Psychol Rev*, "Where is the child's environment?"; Plomin, "Blueprint").

Reading: there is real, long-term, RCT-backed benefit for programs delivered at ages 10-14 (SFP 10-14, FCU). The pattern is *prevention of risk behavior* via family relationship and communication skills, delivered with school/community channels. Engagement is the main challenge.

## 6. Developmental shift: directing to coaching

- **Conflict.** Laursen, Coy and Collins 1998, *Child Development* 69(3), meta-analysis of 24 studies (only 3 observational). Conflict frequency declines from early to mid to late adolescence, but affective intensity rises from early to mid adolescence. So conflict is a rough early-to-mid-teen period (roughly 12-15), and the problem is emotional intensity, not volume. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9680687 (search-confirmed) [S]
- **Monitoring becomes disclosure.** Stattin and Kerr 2000 (above) implies the skill shifts from "watch" to "be someone the teen tells". [S]
- **Autonomy support.** Self-Determination Theory work by Soenens and Vansteenkiste (autonomy support vs psychological control; need-supportive parenting). Not searched this session. [M] Verify, e.g., Soenens and Vansteenkiste 2010, *Developmental Review* 30:74-99.
- **Skills needed for teens differ:** negotiation, disclosure-building, conflict de-escalation, digital/social-media rules, recognizing mental-health problems. Supported indirectly by Dahl 2018 (mental health as a priority), Stattin and Kerr, Laursen 1998. [S]/[M]

## 7. Conclusion and pitch language

**Is 0-12 a defensible core?** Yes. Attachment (Sroufe), the best-tested behavioral parent training (McCart 2006), and the values-transmission literature (Bengtson, Jennings) all support heavy parent influence in this band. Programs for ages 2-11 are effective at any age within that range (Gardner 2019).

**Case for extending to 13-18:** Strong, but with a different mechanic.
1. Adolescence is a high-stakes second window for mental health and risk (Dahl 2018), although "sensitive period" is contested (Fuhrmann 2015).
2. Family connectedness remains protective for teens (Resnick 1997), and parental knowledge via disclosure predicts less delinquency (Stattin and Kerr 2000).
3. RCT-backed teen-parent programs exist (SFP 10-14, Family Check-Up). The long-term effects are on substance use, and engagement is the weak spot.
4. Skills needed (conflict, negotiation, disclosure, digital, mental health) are not a continuation of 0-12 content.

**Suggested structure.**

| Band | Positioning |
|---|---|
| 0-5 / 6-12 | Core: "directing" parenting skills, habits, regulation, values. |
| 10-15 | Priority extension: "bridge to teens". Early adolescence (10-14) is where Dahl 2018 and SFP 10-14 point, and conflict intensity peaks (Laursen 1998). |
| 16-18 | Optional, lower priority. Almost no program evidence retrieved for 16-18 (gap), and parent leverage declines. Cover only as "launch" (autonomy, mental health, driving, substances). |

**Pitch (2-3 sentences):** "The first twelve years set the foundation, and the evidence for parent influence there is the strongest (Sroufe; Gardner 2019). But adolescence is a second high-stakes period in which the parent role changes from directing to coaching: teen disclosure, not surveillance, predicts safer behavior (Stattin and Kerr 2000), and family connectedness protects against nearly every health risk (Resnick 1997). We cover 0-12 as the core and add a distinct Teens track, because the skills differ and parents are most unprepared for them."

**Best 3-4 citations for the pitch:**
1. Gardner et al. 2019, *Child Dev*, doi:10.1111/cdev.13138 (parenting programs work across ages 2-11, so no "too late" cliff).
2. Resnick et al. 1997, *JAMA*, doi:10.1001/jama.1997.03550100049038.
3. Stattin and Kerr 2000, *Child Dev*, doi:10.1111/1467-8624.00210.
4. Dahl et al. 2018, *Nature*, doi:10.1038/nature25770 (with Fuhrmann et al. 2015 as the caveat).

## Evidence gaps to fill before external use

- Harvard Center page wording for the "1 million connections per second" claim.
- Primary teen-survey data (Pew 2023/24, Gallup, Common Sense, Responsibility.org/Roper); none verified.
- Effect sizes and dropout for Parenting Wisely, SFP 10-14 (original RCT), Teen Triple P RCTs.
- 16-18 program evidence; any evidence on whether parent training effects *decrease* with age in adolescent-only samples.
- Kaminski 2008, Soenens/Vansteenkiste, Grusec and Goodnow, Steinberg and Blakemore books: [M] only.
