## 8. What is unique about the US

All from memory / secondary, not number-verified:
- Cherlin "capstone model": marriage as a prestige marker you earn after career/finances are in place; non-college adults increasingly have kids outside marriage. [SEC] https://wiki.krisyotam.com/content/wikipedia_en_all_nopic_2026-03/Capstone_model_of_marriage ; https://www.nationalreview.com/the-agenda/marriage-rests-thin-reed-its-prestige-reihan-salam/amp/
- Finkel "All-or-Nothing Marriage" (2014 NYT op-ed, 2017 book): best marriages are better than ever, but expectation of self-expression/fulfillment needs resources (time, attention) most couples lack; class divide. [SEC] https://news.northwestern.edu/stories/2014/02/opinion-nyt-finkel-marriage ; https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2014/the-all-or-nothing-marriage/
- Cherlin "marriage-go-round" (US has unusually high rates of both marriage and divorce plus repartnering): [E], not verified this session.

Product framing: because modern marriage asks one partner to be best friend, co-CEO and co-parent, and because the main resource missing in the baby years is time, a product that gives small structured skill reps (not hours of therapy) fits the Finkel thesis.

## 10. Implications for the product

### Strongest pitch numbers (all verified unless noted)
1. 52% of two-parent households have both parents working full time (Pew, 2025). [SEC]
2. ~Two-thirds of couples (Gottman: 67%) report a drop in relationship satisfaction in the first ~3 years after baby. [SEC]
3. Couples wait ~6 years before getting help. [SEC]
4. Women initiate ~two-thirds of divorces; within marriage wives report lower quality than husbands. [S] Rosenfeld
5. Mothers of under-6s: 7.5 hrs/day childcare vs 5.3 for fathers (Pew 2023). [SEC]
6. In "egalitarian" marriages, husbands still get ~3.5 more hours/week of leisure and wives do ~2.5 more hours housework. [S] Pew 2023
7. Lack of commitment (75%), infidelity (60%), conflict (~58%) top reasons for divorce among PREP participants. [S] Scott 2013
8. ~40% of US births are to unmarried parents; only ~1/3 of unmarried parents still together at child age 5 (Fragile Families). [S]/[SEC]
9. 1.8M divorces in 2023, refined rate 14.4 per 1,000 married women (down from 22.6 in 1980) - the good-news counter: couples who marry are staying together more. [S] Pew
10. Cognitive labor (anticipating and monitoring) is where women do most; deciding is roughly equal (Daminger 2019). [S]

### Best intervention moments
- Pre-baby (pregnancy) - Gottman/Bringing Baby Home logic; the dip starts ~4-6 months postpartum and meta-analysis shows the main drop between pregnancy and 12 months, smaller 12-24 months. Strongest "prepare" hook; cheapest to change division of labor before roles ossify.
- First year (months 4-12) - the acute pain point: sleep, intimacy, mental load. High motivation.
- Toddler (1-3 yrs) - parenting-style disagreements; "second dip" (small decline 12-24 months).
- Unmarried/cohabiting parents - highest risk of union dissolution; do not require "marriage" language.

### Messaging angles
- Women: "You're not the project manager of the family" - name the mental load (anticipate/monitor), give a visible, shareable system; fairness without nagging.
- Men: "You're not failing; you lack a playbook" - skill-based/game framing, short reps, quick wins, avoid blame; men are reluctant for therapy (stigma not verified here) so position as training, not counseling.
- Couple-level: "Parenting is a team sport, not a test of who is the better parent." Co-op mechanics, shared XP, repair rituals after conflict (contempt -> appreciation; Gottman horsemen antidotes).
- Honest framing: kids are not the sole cause of decline (Mitnick: non-parents decline too); do not overclaim "kids ruin marriages."

### Product design implications
1. Couple-synced mechanics aimed at cognitive labor (shared task visibility, "who noticed it" vs "who did it").
2. Conflict-repair micro-lessons keyed to the four horsemen.
3. Onboarding stage-tuned (expecting / 0-12 mo / 1-3 yrs) and relationship-status neutral (married/cohab).
4. Early-help positioning vs the 6-year wait: low stigma, 5-minute habits.
5. Risk: not a substitute for therapy; add DV/safety screening and referral (DV is a listed divorce reason in Scott 2013).

## 11. Unverified / corrected claims (do not put on slides without checking)
- Online meeting ~50-60%: PNAS verified 39% (2017 hetero), 65% same-sex. Newer figure unverified.
- Median marriage age 30.2/28.6 outdated: Census 2025 = 30.8/28.4.
- "~90% of couples decline" (Doss 2009): not found; use mean-effect wording.
- Premarital education "~1/3" and "31% lower divorce odds": not confirmed.
- Divorce cost $15-20K: surveyed average $11.3K (2019), contested $23K+.
- 18% stay-at-home dads: that is share of at-home parents; 7% of fathers.
- Couples therapy usage %, median marriage duration at divorce, lifetime ~40%, prenups, interracial/same-sex numbers, religion, marriage-go-round: not researched or unconfirmed.
- Gottman 90% accuracy: retrospective fit; caveat when citing.
