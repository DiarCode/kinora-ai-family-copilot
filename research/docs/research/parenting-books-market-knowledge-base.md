## 0. Verdict (TL;DR)

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is there demand for "books distilled into action"? | Yes. Book-summary apps are a proven paid category (Blinkist ~EUR 53M FY23/24 revenue; Headway large, quiz-funnel). |
| Is "we processed 100 books so you don't have to" a good headline? | Partly. Strong on convenience, weak on trust (contradictory experts, evidence quality, endorsement/legal risk). Reframe as "expert consensus engine" (Section 5). |
| Legal risk of ingesting books? | Manageable if: books legally purchased, output is original paraphrase of ideas/methods (not expression), short or no verbatim quotes, no author names/likenesses as endorsement. Pirated copies are the real danger. |
| Biggest strategic risk | Commodity: any LLM can summarize a book. Moat must be couple-sync, gamification, spaced practice, expert-consensus mapping, and (ideally) licensed expert partnerships. |

Fair reading [E]: the "people don't finish/apply books" thesis is intuitively right and the Hawking Index is a fun anecdote, but rigorous completion data for parenting books was not found. Structured, practice-based, delivered-in-small-doses formats (online programs) do have RCT support at moderate effect sizes; that is the evidence to lean on, not "books don't work." Pitch it as "applying > reading," not "books are useless."

Do NOT claim "d=0.65" for the product. Spaced repetition / retrieval practice evidence for behavior change was not researched in this pass (the learning-science literature is for knowledge retention; transfer to parenting behavior is an assumption [E]).

## 4. Legal

### 4.1 Copyright basics [E, standard US law; consult counsel]
- Ideas, facts, methods, systems are not protected (17 U.S.C. 102(b)); expression is. Parenting techniques ("name the feeling, set the limit") are ideas/methods. Distinctive phrasing, named frameworks' text, examples, scripts, and structure are expression.
- Abridgments/summaries that track the structure and expression of a book can be derivative works (17 U.S.C. 101, 106(2)). Short original paraphrase of ideas is the safe lane; chapter-by-chapter close paraphrase is the grey lane.
- Practical comparables: getAbstract licenses; Blinkist/Shortform/Instaread operate on paraphrase/fair-use theory. No lawsuit against Blinkist or Shortform found in search. [SEC] https://www.producthunt.com/questions/is-blinkist-legal (forum, weak) ; Instaread debate at Jane Friedman link above. Authors have publicly complained about summary apps hurting sales. [SEC] inkl link "book summary apps accused of damaging authors sales". Whether Blinkist licenses "most" titles: NOT verified.

### 4.3 What this means for us [E]
- Our use differs from "training": we would run RAG/prompting over book text to produce outputs for paying users. The market-substitution factor (Ross; Kadrey dilution) is the main exposure: if the app output substitutes for buying the book, risk goes up. Summaries inherently risk this.
- Pirated copies (LibGen etc.) are the proven liability driver (Bartz). Buy legit copies (or use licensed/open sources); keep purchase receipts/provenance log.
- Safest architecture: use books only to extract an internal, human-reviewed structured "principles/skills" ontology (idea-level: skill, steps, age range, when to use, evidence grade). Show users our own original wording; never store/serve passages. Prefer primary research (RCTs, AAP/CDC guidance, public-domain/government sources) as the content spine, books as secondary "see also."
- Output guardrails: block verbatim >~ a sentence (or none), no chapter-order mimicry, no named-framework reproductions (e.g., full "acronym" frameworks) beyond name + one-line description.
- Licensing option: approach publishers/authors (many parenting experts run courses/apps and want distribution). Affiliate "buy the book" links = goodwill + revenue and reduce substitution argument.

### 4.4 Trademark / endorsement (Lanham Act s.43(a)) [E]
- Naming an author to cite a work is nominative/descriptive use if neutral and necessary. "Based on Dr. Becky's method," author photos, logos, quotes-as-testimonials, or "approved by" imply sponsorship -> false endorsement / right-of-publicity risk (state law).
- Do: "Draws on principles from 100+ books and 300+ studies"; neutral bibliography ("Sources: Siegel & Bryson, The Whole-Brain Child, 2011"). Don't: author likenesses, book cover art in ads (covers are copyrighted/trademarked), "official," "as taught by," implying partnership, using names as keywords in ads without counsel review.
- Also: health-ish claims -> FTC substantiation. "Backed by research" needs real support; avoid promising child outcomes. Not a medical/therapy product; add disclaimers and escalate-to-professional paths. Children's data (COPPA) if kids ever use the app.

## 5. Marketing claim analysis

| Frame | Appeal | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| "We processed 100 books so you don't have to" | Convenience, anti-overwhelm (58% of millennial parents overwhelmed) | Highlights copying (legal optics: "we ingested 100 books"), implies authors' work is replaceable (author backlash/BookTok), "so you don't have to" = substitution = market-harm narrative; contradictions between experts make a single "top suggestions" list dishonest; pop books != evidence |
| Recommended: "Expert Consensus Engine" | Honest, differentiated, research-forward | Needs real methodology |

Proposed framing: "Where experts agree, where they differ - and one small thing to try tonight." Copy options: "100+ books and 300+ studies, boiled down to what actually agrees." Avoid "so you don't have to read" (or soften: "so you can start today").

Consensus map [E, to be validated by your content team]:
- Agree: authoritative style (warmth + consistent limits); emotion coaching/validation; special one-on-one time; repair after rupture; routines/predictability; modeling.
- Differ: sleep training vs attachment-style approaches; screen-time specifics (Haidt-style strict limits vs more permissive views); discipline details (time-outs, consequences); gentle vs authoritative framing.
UI: badges "Experts agree" / "Experts differ" / "Evidence: RCT / observational / expert opinion". Show tradeoffs and let the couple choose a stance (couple-sync feature: surface disagreement between partners, not only between experts).

## 6. Product design

- Atomic unit: a 5-minute "practice card" = situation cue (if-then plan: "If my child melts down at bedtime, then I will name the feeling in one sentence"), one script/action, 30-second reflection, partner nudge. Rooted in implementation intentions (modest effect; see Section 2).
- Spacing: reintroduce skill after 1, 3, 7, 14 days with new contexts (retrieval prompt + real-life attempt). Evidence for behavior transfer is assumed [E].
- Gamification: streaks, couple "License" levels, shared goals; avoid shaming for parents.
- "Book-to-Action": user picks a book they own -> 14-day practice plan.
  - Safer variant: user selects title; app generates plan from OUR own skill library mapped to that book's topics (no book text used); optional user-entered highlights/notes ("your notes -> your plan") processed only for that user; no uploading of full books/ebook files (users uploading copies they own is still a copying grey area; avoid). Offer "buy the book" affiliate link for non-owners.
  - Riskier variant: ingesting the book's text and re-expressing chapters as a plan = derivative-work exposure; do only with license.
- "Expert Library": neutral attribution (author, title, year), our own 1-2 sentence idea descriptions, evidence grade, affiliate buy links, no photos/covers without license.
- Hackathon scope [E]: ship consensus map for ~10 skills + couple-sync + 14-day plan generator from a curated skill library; stub book picker with 20 titles.
