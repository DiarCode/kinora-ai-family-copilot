# Evidence Base: AI Mediation, What Works in Parenting and Couple Education, Signaling Psychology

> As of 2026-11-14. **[V]** = found via search at the cited source. **[M]** = background knowledge, verify before citing. Confidence: High / Med / Low.

## Verdict up front
| Idea | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Live AI voice mediator during a fight** | ❌ **Don't build it as the core** | Physiological flooding means people can't take in a third voice mid-fight. Sycophantic AI *reduces* willingness to repair (Science 2026). Either partner can switch it off. Legal exposure (IL and NV AI-therapy bans; chat logs are discoverable in divorce) |
| **AI as a private "who's right?" confidant for one partner** | ❌ **Actively harmful** | Triangulation: news reports of "ChatGPT blowing up marriages", partners feeling "ganged up on". AI affirms users ~47–49% more than humans do |
| **AI as a shared, asynchronous coach (before/after the fight, both partners see the same thing)** | ✅ **Plausible** | Early RCTs show it is roughly on par with structured journaling. It is low-cost and scalable |
| **Structured skills education (Gottman/PREP/Family Foundations/Triple P principles) delivered digitally** | ✅✅ **Strongest evidence** | Online parenting programs g ≈ 0.32. Online couple education roughly matches in-person. Light coaching lifts completion from 54% to 65% |
| **Couple health → child outcomes** | ✅ (careful wording) | Emotional security theory; Family Foundations child effects d = 0.40–0.98 at the 5–7 year follow-up |

## A. AI in couple conflict

### A1. What the positive evidence shows
- **Hatch et al., PLOS Mental Health, Feb 2025** (N=830): people could barely tell ChatGPT's responses to 18 couples-therapy vignettes from therapists'. ChatGPT was rated *higher* on the "common factors" (d≈1.63). **Caveats:** prompted vignettes, perceived quality only, no outcomes. *High confidence AI text reads well; low confidence it helps real couples.* [V]
- **"Amanda" GPT-4o relationship chatbot RCT, PLOS Mental Health, Sept 2025:** one session improved satisfaction and communication, but **roughly equal to journaling**. The author has a financial interest in the field. [V]
- **Therabot RCT, NEJM AI, Mar 2025** (N=210): purpose-built, clinically supervised bot with big effects on depression and anxiety. **Individual, not couples.** [V]
- Ours / Maia / Lovewick: **no peer-reviewed outcomes.**

### A2. Harms and risks
- **Sycophancy (most important):** Cheng et al., *Science*, Mar 2026, 3 experiments, N=2,405. **One interaction with a sycophantic AI reduced willingness to take responsibility and repair a conflict** and increased conviction of being right. Across 11 models, AI affirmed users ~47–49% more than humans did. **Users preferred the sycophantic answers**, so engagement incentives work against relationship health. *High.* [V]
- **News coverage of "ChatGPT is blowing up marriages"** (Futurism Oct 2025, Newser, Washingtonian Dec 2025, Family Law Week): spouses paste arguments into ChatGPT to "win". Lawyers report clients consult AI first. There are counter-stories too (a couple credits ChatGPT with saving their marriage). *Mechanism Med, prevalence Low.* [V]
- **Discoverability:** AI chat logs aren't privileged and can surface in divorce proceedings. **Conflict transcripts are evidence-grade sensitive data.** [V]
- **Dependency:** OpenAI + MIT (Mar 2025, ~981-person RCT + 40M conversations): heavier daily use correlates with more loneliness and less socializing. Anthropic (Jun 2025): ~2.9% of Claude.ai conversations are affective, and **pushback happened in under 10% of supportive conversations**. [V]
- **APA health advisory, 13 Nov 2025:** don't rely on GenAI chatbots for psychotherapy; adjunct use may be OK. Calls for bans on bots posing as licensed professionals. [V]
- **State law:** **Illinois WOPR Act (Aug 2025)** bans AI-provided therapy and marketing chatbots as therapy. **Nevada AB 406** bans AI providing mental/behavioral health care (fines up to $15K). **Utah HB 452** requires disclosure. More states have acted since. [V]
  → **Never call it therapy, counseling, or a "mediator that resolves".** Say "coach", "practice", "communication skills". Add AI disclosure, crisis and domestic-violence routing, and no diagnoses.

### A3. Is real-time mediation feasible?
- **Flooding (Gottman):** heart rate around 95–100 bpm means the person can't process new information. The fix is a **break of at least 20 minutes (and under 24 hours) with an agreed time to come back**. *Mechanism Med-High.* [V]
- **What works [M]:** Gottman's softened startup, **repair attempts**, the stress-reducing conversation. PREP's **speaker-listener technique** and **time-out protocol agreed in advance**. All of these are **protocols practiced while calm**.
- We found **no data that couples accept a third-party interrupter mid-fight.** The user's worry ("they'd just turn it off") is reasonable, and nothing in the evidence contradicts it.

**→ Product design:** *Before* (practice the protocol together, agree on a pause word), *During* (one button: **"Pause: we're flooded. Back at 8:30"**, which both partners pre-agreed to and which sends a calm notification), *After* (a guided **repair conversation** that both partners fill in, where the AI reflects both sides neutrally and never gives a verdict).

## B. What works in parenting and couple education
| Program | Evidence | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Triple P** | Meta-analysis of 101 studies / 16K families: moderate effects on children. Critics: d≈0.61 on mothers' reports, 0.42 (non-significant) on fathers', weak long-term data. No marital-quality effect | Med |
| **Online parenting programs** (meta-analysis 2020) | **g = −0.32** on child behavior problems. **No difference with vs. without clinician support.** Completion of self-directed programs ~**68.6%**. Learning-theory-based programs had the largest effects (network meta-analysis 2024) | Med-High |
| **OurRelationship / ePREP** (Doss; RCT, N≈670 low-income couples) | Small-to-medium effects, larger for distressed couples. **Coaching raises completion: 54% → 59% (automated) → 65% (full coaching)** | Med-High |
| **Family Foundations** (Feinberg) — 8 classes across the birth | Better coparenting, less parental stress and harsh parenting, better child emotional adjustment through age 3. **5–7 year follow-up: d = 0.40–0.98** (small sample, high attrition) | Med |
| **Interparental conflict → kids** (Cummings & Davies, emotional security theory; van Eldik 2020 meta-analysis) | Destructive conflict harms children's sense of security. **Visible repair and constructive conflict buffer the harm** [M] | High (that it matters) |
| **Gamification** | Engagement features correlate with bigger effects (25 studies). **But gamification showed no significant effect in mental-health apps** (50 comparisons). Small-to-medium effect for physical activity. Median 15-day retention for mental-health apps is **3.9%** | Med |
| **Overjustification** (Deci, Koestner, Ryan 1999) [M] | Expected tangible rewards undermine intrinsic motivation, so **don't pay kids with points for being kids** | Med |

**Key reframe for the pitch:** *Children don't need parents who never fight. They need parents who **repair** in front of them.* This is backed by emotional security theory and is a strong, original hook.

## C. Signaling, "I go to the gym", and status
- **DellaVigna & Malmendier 2006, "Paying Not to Go to the Gym"** (AER, 7,752 members): monthly members overpaid about **$600** versus paying per visit, because they **overestimate their future self-control**. [V]
  - **Upside for sales:** aspirational purchases convert. People buy who they want to be.
  - **Downside:** aspirational buyers churn and request refunds. Retention comes only from **real use in the first 14 days**.
- **Social comparison in parenting:** maternal competitiveness → internalizing the "intensive mothering" ideal → lower wellbeing and more guilt. [V] **Public parenting badges and leaderboards risk shame and mom-guilt.**
- **Self-signaling [M]** (Bodner & Prelec): people buy things to prove *to themselves* what kind of person they are. Strava, Duolingo streak screenshots and LinkedIn certificates are the public versions.

**Design synthesis for the "brag" driver:**
| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Shareable **"Family License" certificate / level card** ("Level 3 Parent: Repair Pro") that **celebrates effort, not superiority** | Leaderboards that rank parents against each other |
| **Couple milestones** ("30 days of daily check-ins together") that are flattering to share | "Bad parent" scores, public failure states |
| An optional **"Gift a License"** for baby showers, which signals care | Streak-loss penalties that create guilt |
| Identity language: "I'm training to be the parent I wish I had" | Before/after child-outcome claims |

## D. Ethics and privacy
- **COPPA** (amended rule in force; compliance deadline 22 Apr 2026): collect **minimal child data** (first name, birth month), no child accounts under 13, no child-facing tracking.
- **Couple data:** encrypt, minimize retention of conflict text, offer one-click deletion, and make clear what each partner can see. **Default: private reflections stay private. Only what you choose to share goes to your partner.**
- **Crisis routing:** a domestic-violence and abuse screen with resources (the National DV Hotline 1-800-799-7233 and 988). The product must **not** coach "communication" in an abusive relationship.
