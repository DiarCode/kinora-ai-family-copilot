## 1. Is there a US "parent committee" (родительский комитет)?

**Short answer: not as one tight body. The US has loose layers instead.**

| | **CIS parent committee** | **US equivalents** |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | One committee + one chat per class; the same 25–30 kids for years | **PTA/PTO** (school-wide) + a per-class **"room parent"** (class email list, parties, teacher gifts) |
| Frequency | Meets every term; the chat is active daily | PTA meetings monthly or quarterly. **83%** of parents attended at least one general school/PTA meeting in 2022–23 (down from 89%) [S, NCES]. **72%** attended a parent-teacher conference [S] |
| Cohesion | High: a fixed cohort, everyone knows everyone | **Weaker.** Looser and more school-wide. (Our claim that classes are reshuffled yearly in elementary school is *not verified*; ask a few US parents) |
| Channel | WhatsApp/Telegram class chat | School apps: **ClassDojo** (claims use in **95% of US schools**), ParentSquare, Remind; email lists; SignUpGenius; some class text/GroupMe threads |
| Membership | Everyone in the class | National PTA fell from ~12M members (1960s) to under 5M by 2012. Independent PTOs cover roughly 75% of schools [SEC, old] |

**For our entry segment (expecting + kids 0–6), school isn't the committee yet.** The real "committees" for young-kid parents are:

| Where US parents of young kids swap experience | Size / signal |
|---|---|
| **Online communities** (Reddit, Facebook "Moms of [town]" groups, Peanut) | **34%** of parents of kids ≤12 visit online parenting communities **monthly**; **mothers 42% vs fathers 22%** [S, Pew Nov 2025]. **r/Parenting has 8M+ members, and 23% of its posts are about dividing childcare** [S, Pew], which is exactly our pain |
| **Daycare / preschool pickup and parent chats** | Daily contact with the same families for years. The closest thing to a CIS cohort |
| **Baby registry** | **Babylist: used by 22% of US families having a baby**, 10M+ gift buyers, $750M revenue in 2025 [SEC, company] |
| **Mom groups** (MomCo/MOPS, Hello Mamas, PEPS, hospital new-parent groups, La Leche League) | MomCo: **2,075 groups, ~47K moms** [S] |
| **Faith communities** | **43%** of parents say their kids attend religious services monthly [S, Pew] |
| Neighborhood (Nextdoor, HOA chats), youth sports sidelines, birthday parties, playgrounds | Weaker, but useful for kids aged 4+ |
| Workplace (parent ERGs, coworkers) | Exists, but no good data. The employer channel risks breaking the **B2C-only** rule |

**Word of mouth works:** **89%** trust recommendations from people they know (Nielsen 2021) [S].

**Pitch line:** *"In the CIS every class has a parent committee. In the US, the committee is a daycare pickup line, a Facebook group and r/Parenting, where 1 in 4 posts is about who does more childcare. We plug into those, and every purchase brings a teammate."*

## 2. Are women the drivers, and do men "just go along"?

**Answer: half right.** Women are more often the **first movers**. But "men just submit" is **not supported** by the evidence. Men self-start when the framing fits them and when they get their **own role**.

| Evidence | Number |
|---|---|
| Monthly use of online parenting communities | **Moms 42% vs dads 22%** [S, Pew 2025] |
| Fathers' share of parenting-program participants | Often low: **~21% in Triple P**, 6–25% across studies [S, Panter-Brick 2014]. Mostly because programs were **designed for moms** |
| Who first recognizes the problem and seeks couples therapy | Wives in **~67–73%** of cases (2003 study; a 2018 study found a smaller gap) [SEC] |
| Past-year therapy use | Men **10.9%** vs women **16.9%** [S, CDC] |
| **Dads who want more time with their kids** | **63%** say they spend too little time (vs 35% of moms) [S, Pew] |
| **Dads who feel judged by their partner** at least sometimes | **56%** (vs 49% of moms) [S, Pew 2023] |
| Dads saying they do a "very good job" | 39% (vs 51% of moms) [S, Pew 2015]. **A confidence gap** |
| New dads taking under 2 weeks of leave or none | **64%** (one-state sample) [SEC] · paternal postpartum depression **~10%** [SEC] |
| Men with no close friends | **15%** (up from 3% in 1990) [S] → **dad-to-dad word of mouth ("hey Bob…") is weaker than mom-to-mom** |

**What this means:**
- Expected first purchasers ≈ **65–75% women / 25–35% men** [E]. With a dedicated dad funnel, a 60/40 split is a realistic stretch.
- Men's motivation is real (they want more time, feel less confident and feel judged), but **therapy framing turns them off** and **mom-centric design pushes them out**. In the Cowans' "Supporting Father Involvement" trials, **couples groups worked better than fathers-only groups**. That validates our couple-team format.
- **"Maternal gatekeeping"** (Allen & Hawkins; Schoppe-Sullivan): moms can encourage *or* inhibit dad involvement. If the app makes mom the boss who assigns dad tasks, dad disengages, and the mental load on mom goes up.
- The "hey Bob" referral works less often among men than among women. Men are more **online-review and content driven** (podcasts, YouTube, Reddit). The strongest male entry point is **his own partner's invite, framed as his role, not her request**.

**Product and marketing moves:**
1. **Either partner can buy and invite.** The invited partner is a co-owner with his own track, not a guest.
2. **A separate dad funnel:** "Dad License", "game plan", "training", "rep", "coach", built on competence and "be the dad you want to be". **Never** "fix your marriage" or "therapy".
3. **Invite copy written for him:** *"Jess started your team's Family License. Your first 5-minute rep: the 2-minute bedtime handoff."* Not "your wife thinks you need this."
4. **Equal credit**, with no "mom assigns, dad executes" mechanics.
5. **Dad channels:** YouTube and podcast sponsorships (dad and self-improvement shows), r/daddit, sports-season creatives. **Test competitive gamification** (team vs. team challenges) on dads before committing. The evidence is mixed.

## 3. The parenting-books market and the "we processed 100 books" idea

### 3.1 Market
| Fact | Number |
|---|---|
| US self-help print units | **+14.7% in 2025**, driven by Mel Robbins' *The Let Them Theory* (2.8M+ copies), then **−26.3% in Q1 2026** [SEC, Publishers Weekly]. **Blockbuster-driven and volatile.** Don't size our market on book units |
| US audiobooks | **$2.43B revenue in 2025 (+9%)**; 58% of Americans have listened to one [SEC] |
| Parenting mega-hits | Haidt's *The Anxious Generation* (2M+ copies, per publisher copy) [SEC]; *Good Inside*, *The Whole-Brain Child*, *How to Talk So Kids Will Listen*, *Hunt, Gather, Parent*, Emily Oster's books (sales not public) |
| Overwhelm | **58%** of millennial parents feel overwhelmed by online advice, yet 63% find it helpful [SEC] |
| **"Books distilled into action" apps prove people pay** | **Blinkist** ~€53M revenue (FY23/24; bought by Go1 in 2023, reportedly bought back by its founders in 2026). **Headway** ~$90/yr (the same quiz-funnel model as Zimran). **Shortform** ~$170–200/yr. **getAbstract** ~$299/yr (licenses its content) |
| Parenting-specific book-to-action app | **None found** (only one search; verify in the app stores before claiming it in the deck) |

### 3.2 "People buy books but don't apply them": is it true?
- **Partly.** Pew (Oct 2025): **75%** of US adults read at least part of a book in the past year, flat since 2021. People aren't "lazy readers", so **don't pitch "books are useless"**.
- The real gap is between **knowing and doing**: Kindle-highlight data (Ellenberg's "Hawking Index") shows nonfiction highlights cluster in the early chapters.
- **Implementation intentions** (if-then plans) help turn intentions into action. Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 reported d≈0.65; bias-corrected updates suggest **d≈0.15–0.35**. Say "small-to-moderate".
- **Practice-based** parenting programs show moderate effects (d≈0.47, shrinking at long follow-up).
- → **Pitch: "Applying beats reading."** Books give knowledge; we give **5-minute reps, if-then plans, and a partner who does it with you.**

### 3.3 Legal: can we load 100 books into a knowledge base?
| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| **Buy legitimate copies** and keep a provenance log | **Pirated or shadow-library copies.** In *Bartz v. Anthropic*, training on lawfully bought books was fair use, but pirated copies were not, leading to the **$1.5B settlement** (approved July 2026) |
| Extract an **internal skill map** (ideas, methods and facts aren't copyrightable) and write **all user-facing content in our own words** | Serve book passages, close chapter-by-chapter paraphrase, or mimic a book's structure (risk of a derivative work) |
| **Lead with primary research** (RCTs, AAP/CDC guidance) and use books as secondary support | "So you don't have to read it" marketing. It signals **market substitution**, the exact theory the judge invited in *Kadrey v. Meta* |
| Cite works neutrally ("Further reading") + **affiliate "buy the book" links** (goodwill, revenue, the opposite of substitution) | Author photos, book covers, logos, "based on Dr. Becky's method". That's **Lanham Act false-endorsement** risk |
| Consider **licensing or partnering** with 1–2 authors (getAbstract licenses) | Letting users upload full ebooks |
| Substantiate outcome claims (FTC) | "Guaranteed better kids" claims |

Other cases: *Thomson Reuters v. Ross* found AI use was not fair use (affirmed by the Third Circuit in Sept 2026; narrow, not generative AI). The OpenAI cases have no merits ruling yet.

### 3.4 Better framing: the **"Expert Consensus Engine"**
Replace *"We read 100 books so you don't have to"* with:

> **"Where experts agree, where they differ, and one 5-minute thing to try tonight."**
> *Draws on principles from 100+ books and 300+ studies.*

| Badge | Examples |
|---|---|
| 🟢 **Experts agree** | Warmth + consistent limits (authoritative style), emotion coaching, 1:1 "special time", **repair after conflict**, praising effort |
| 🟡 **Experts differ** | Sleep training vs responsive settling, exact screen-time limits, time-outs vs time-ins |
| 🔬 **Evidence grade** | RCT / observational / expert opinion |

**Why it's stronger:**
1. It's **honest**. Experts really do contradict each other, which is why 58% of millennial parents feel overwhelmed.
2. It's **legally safer**.
3. It sets up a **unique couple feature**: *"Experts differ on sleep training, and so do you two. Here's a 10-minute Team Huddle to pick your family's approach."* **No book or summary app can do this.** It turns expert disagreement into a team decision, our core skill.

## 4. What changes in the pitch and product
1. **Add a GTM slide:** partner invite + license card → mom groups/Reddit → daycare codes → Class Pack via room parents → registry/OB-GYN. Opener: *"The US has no parent committee, so we built one into the app: your team."*
2. **Dad funnel + co-owner invite.** Our answer to "will dads use it?": the 63% who want more time, the 56% who feel judged, and the couples-over-fathers-only evidence.
3. **Expert Consensus Engine** replaces "100 books summarized". Add the "Experts agree / Experts differ" badges to lessons, and the **"we differ too" Team Huddle** feature.
4. **The knowledge base** is built from legally bought books + research → our own skill map → original micro-lessons, with affiliate links to the books.
